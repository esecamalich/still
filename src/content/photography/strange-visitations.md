## Español

Proyecto en curso, iniciado tras reconocer las limitaciones sugestivas de mi trabajo anterior, Lost (in) Paradise (2021-2022), y la manera en como este reproducía, de forma involuntaria, algunos de los clichés que han dominado la representación de Bali desde su etapa colonial. 

La fase inicial del proyecto surgió a partir de la observación más atenta de los símbolos, señas y sistemas visuales por los cuales la religión, cultura y tradición Balinesa oscila entre lo ritual y lo ordinario. Ofrendas, patrones sagrados y atuendos ceremoniales, conviviendo con peluquerías, puestos de comida y talleres.

Estas observaciones naturalmente evolucionaron hacia cuestionamientos más amplios sobre los aspectos que dan forma al Bali moderno, particularmente: migración, turismo, política e identidad. 

Reconociendo mi doble posicionamiento como testigo y participante activo dentro de estas dinámicas, Strange Visitations se encuentra en una etapa de consolidación, orientada a definir su estructura final, su ritmo narrativo y sus decisiones formales, con el objetivo de cerrar el proyecto de manera crítica y coherente.

## Origins

The first time I ever thought about Strange Visitations was after having come back to Bali for a second time since I moved during the COVID pandemic. 

I had already self-published my first book, Lost (in) Paradise, and was well aware of the places where I had been too shy, where I had restrained my eye in the hopes of publishing an agreeable work that people would appreciate.

For the most part, I wanted to distance myself from the traditional ways in which Bali tends to be portrayed.

The beginning of my research was mostly graphical, photographing the signs and symbols through which Balinese religion, culture and tradition could be interpreted. 

Offerings, sacred patterns and ceremonial attires, living alongside petrol pumps, barbershops and food carts. 

These initial observations were what I called "strange visitations", a concept taken from the book "Sekala & Niskala", by Fred B. Eisman Jr. Given how I had no choice over what caught my attention, I realized I was as captive to my sources of inspiration as the people I saw falling into a trance during Balinese ceremonies.

I then moved to the outskirts of the ceremonies. What happened during the lull times between prayers, the mundane and the unglamorous. 

My observations naturally took me through a few of the issues modern Bali struggles with:

- Immigration
- Tourism 
- Identity

Throughout this whole time, I kept drawing parallels between Mexico and Bali. Postcolonial countries, worldwide destinations, indigenous struggles.

In the latest phase of the project, acknowledging that I'm as much a witness as I'm an active participant, I've taken my camera straight up into the dynamics spawned from the tourist industry in Ubud, centered around the city's central Art Market.
