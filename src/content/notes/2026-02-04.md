I've been pondering upon the ethics of photographing the street sellers and peddlers, including their kids, offering souvenirs to the tourists in downtown Ubud.

On one side, I'm aware of the debate in the photography community. about whether it's ethical or not to photograph people in disadvantaged positions, or without consent.

On the other side, I question the validity of this questions. Though I understand the sentiment where it's coming from, I wonder where the world would be if photographs like the Napalm Girl wouldn't have been taken because of ethical dilemmas.

Of course, I'm not implying that my work is at the level of Pulitzer-winning photographers, but I'm also aware that my intention is not to capitalize on this people but to paint a full picture of a very real situation happening right now.

I find myself navigating between the feelings arising. A sadness for the hardship I see, mixed with the knowing there's a single person to blame about this. Things are the way they are, and my work is to shine a light on it.
