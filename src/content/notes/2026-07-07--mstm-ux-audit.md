**Purpose**

This document defines the UX & Product Audit service: what problems it solves, who it is for, what clients receive, and how the engagement is structured. Its purpose is to establish clear expectations before the audit begins and provide a repeatable framework for delivering consistent, actionable insights.

---

# 1. Service Goals

## 1.1. Business Goals

- Help businesses identify the highest-impact improvements before investing in design or development.
- Position Make Stuff That Matters as a strategic product partner rather than solely an implementation agency.
- Create a clear entry point for longer-term consulting, UX Blueprint, or implementation work.

## 1.2. Client Goals

Clients should leave the audit with:

- A clear understanding of where users experience friction.
- Confidence about what deserves attention first.
- Prioritized recommendations based on impact and effort.
- An actionable roadmap rather than a list of observations.
- Alignment between user needs and business objectives.

---

# 2. Design Principles

- **Diagnose before prescribing.** Understand the problem before proposing solutions.
- **Prioritize impact over perfection.** Focus on the changes that matter most.
- **Support every recommendation with evidence.** Explain why something is a problem.
- **Think beyond the interface.** UX includes business strategy, content, product decisions, and user expectations.
- **Deliver clarity, not complexity.** Clients should finish the audit knowing exactly what to do next.

---

# 3. UX & Product Audit

## 3.1. Purpose

Evaluate an existing digital product through the lens of user experience, product strategy, usability, accessibility, and business goals to uncover opportunities for meaningful improvement.

---

## 3.2. Success Criteria

A successful audit enables clients to:

- Understand their product's strengths and weaknesses.
- See their product through the eyes of first-time users.
- Identify the biggest sources of friction.
- Prioritize improvements based on business impact.
- Make informed product decisions moving forward.

---

## 3.3. Ideal Clients

The audit is particularly valuable for teams who:

- Feel their product "isn't working" but don't know why.
- Have low conversion rates.
- Receive recurring customer support questions.
- Are planning a redesign.
- Are preparing to build new features.
- Have inherited an existing product.
- Want an expert review before investing in development.

---

## 3.4. Deliverables

Every audit includes:

- Product review
- UX evaluation
- Information architecture review
- Interface review
- Content & messaging review
- Accessibility observations
- Product strategy recommendations
- Prioritized action plan
- Written audit report
- Recorded walkthrough
- Follow-up strategy call

---

## 3.5. Audit Framework

The product is evaluated across several dimensions.

### 3.5.1. User Experience

Evaluate how easily users accomplish their goals.

Areas reviewed include:

- Navigation
- User flows
- Information architecture
- Cognitive load
- Learnability
- Error prevention
- User feedback

---

### 3.5.2. Interface Design

Evaluate how effectively the interface communicates hierarchy and interaction.

Areas reviewed include:

- Visual hierarchy
- Layout
- Typography
- Spacing
- Components
- States
- Consistency

---

### 3.5.3. Content & Communication

Review how effectively the product communicates value.

Areas reviewed include:

- Headlines
- Calls to action
- Microcopy
- Empty states
- Error messages
- Value proposition
- Trust signals

---

### 3.5.4. Product Strategy

Evaluate whether the product supports both user goals and business goals.

Areas reviewed include:

- Feature prioritization
- User onboarding
- Activation
- Conversion opportunities
- Retention opportunities
- Product positioning

---

### 3.5.5. Technical Experience

Review aspects that influence perceived product quality.

Areas reviewed include:

- Performance
- Responsive behaviour
- Broken interactions
- UX implementation issues
- Consistency across devices

---

### 3.5.6. Business Alignment

Review how effectively the experience supports business objectives.

Areas reviewed include:

- Conversion funnels
- Lead generation
- Calls to action
- Pricing presentation
- User confidence
- Decision-making moments

---

## 3.6. Deliverable Structure

The final report is organized into four sections.

### Executive Summary

A high-level overview of the biggest opportunities.

---

### Key Findings

Each issue includes:

- Description
- Why it matters
- Evidence
- Severity
- Recommendation

---

### Prioritization

Recommendations are categorized by:

- High Impact / Low Effort
- High Impact / High Effort
- Low Impact / Low Effort
- Low Impact / High Effort

---

### Next Steps

A practical roadmap for implementation.

---

## 3.7. What the Audit Does Not Include

The audit focuses on diagnosis rather than execution.

It does not include:

- Wireframes
- UI design
- Visual redesign
- Development
- User testing sessions
- Product implementation

These can be addressed in a follow-up UX Blueprint or implementation project.

---

## 3.8. Recommended Next Step

Following the audit, clients typically choose one of three paths:

### Option 1

Implement the recommendations internally.

---

### Option 2

Continue with a UX Blueprint to redesign key flows before implementation.

---

### Option 3

Partner with Make Stuff That Matters to implement the improvements.

# 4. Frequently Asked Questions

## 4.1. What kinds of products can be audited?

Marketing websites, SaaS products, web applications, internal tools, e-commerce platforms, membership sites, and digital services.

---

## 4.2. Do I need analytics?

No. Analytics are helpful but not required.

---

## 4.3. Will you redesign the interface?

No. The audit identifies opportunities and recommends improvements. Design work is part of the UX Blueprint service.

---

## 4.4. Can developers use this report?

Yes. Recommendations are written to support designers, developers, founders, and product teams.

---

## 4.5. How long does the process take?

Depending on product complexity, audits are typically delivered within one to two weeks.

---

## 4.6. What happens after the audit?

Clients may implement the recommendations themselves, continue with a UX Blueprint, or hire Make Stuff That Matters for implementation.
