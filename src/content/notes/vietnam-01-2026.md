# Peeking at the edges of reality

I began the day by looking out of Greg's balcony. Hangover from the previous night's reencounter with my good old friend, I looked at the foggy landscape and fought hard not to romanticize the smog that clouds over Hanoi during winter days.

On my way out of the apartment, a colorful pile of *stuff* greeted me. Of course I had to take a photo of it.

Once out in the street, **I was back in familiar territory.**

---

I didn't have much of a plan other than knowing I wanted to walk. No schedule, no goal. No nothing other than being present with my camera.

A few test shots, making sure that I have my distances right. Stablishing myself in place.

More than ever before, I'm in my "nothing" phase. Entertained by what feels like void, empty spaces. I've got more to learn from looking at that which we take for granted, peeking at the edges of reality, than whatever is expected from a classically beautiful photograph.

---

Hanoi will always hold a special place in my heart, as it was here where my photography shifted. My first photo series, finding pleasure in photographing the same place over and over again.

A decade after, I feel confident in my process. Embodied, dynamic. Able to talk about this place in ways that feel authentic to myself, understanding what before I could barely even glimpse.

---
---

Ever since I first lived here, I was struck by the ease with which whole areas were demolished, giving way to modernity.

This hasn't changed. If anything, the rate at which it happens has increased.

---

Hanoi will always hold a special place in my heart, as the place where a new version of me woke up. 

And though I hesitate to use such words –– to wake up –– for they carry connotations from which I'd rather distance myself, I can clearly see a difference, a decade after I first crossed this path.
