From the beginning of my time on the island, Bali felt like an incubator, a place where all the recourses necessary for the development of those looking to do so, regardless of shape and form, were available.

"Mama Bali" is one of the many nicknames foreigners have given to this place, because of its strict, but loving character, who's womb nourishes but will irremediably push you out into the world, kicking and screaming if needed.

Many times I've heard the stories of people who stubbornly remained here. even after all the signals clearly pointed that it was time for them to leave the safety of Bali's lap, before finally being slapped until they let go. Such is the level of attachment some of us develop in here.

For foreigners who have never experienced a supportive environment such as this, from the privilege of living at the edges of Balinese society, the allure of Bali is placed on top of a romanticized view.

Influenced by more than a century of clever marketing, and looking through the glass of their crystal castles, the tourists who stay –– like myself –– develop a false sense of belonging.

Having made little-to-no-effort to integrate themselves in the daily rituals of the place they now call home, and having realized how demanding these rituals can be, they quickly recede back into their echo chambers. Their hosts becoming more of a curiosity in the background of their lives, or a cheap hand for hire when needed.

It's close to impossible to request the full assimilation of immigrants that come and stay in Bali (or in any other place, for that matter), even though along the way I've met a handful of people who have done it.

In any case, for people like me, "Mama Bali" is more of a close aunt. While she might love, and care, and treat us almost as if we were one of her own children, it's the Balinese themselves who –– for better or worse –– get to stay under her shawl.

As much of an incubator as it felt during my early days, it's important to point out the circumstances that allowed for this to happen: the exploitation of a corrupt system, and a privileged access to circles that most locals could only dream of, as well as many more advantages that the mere status as a foreigner provides.

It's easy to love a place when you're not bounded by its social contracts.

This, of course, is not unique to Bali. Wherever there is an exploit to be benefitted from, there will be people doing so. It's not that what bothers me, but the balls to claim love and ownership of a place most of us are only extracting from.

One's relationship to a place goes beyond the transactional. It demands mutual respect and an equal footing. Compromise. Handing money out and expecting to be served like royalty is nothing but modern colonial mindset.
