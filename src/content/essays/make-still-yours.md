# Make Still Yours

If you've followed this documentation from the beginning, you've done quite a bit.

You installed the theme.

Published your website.

Wrote your first essay.

Learned how the components fit together.

Changed the design.

Hopefully broke a few things.

And, most importantly, realized that none of it was nearly as scary as it first seemed.

That's a wonderful place to be.

## The goal was never to use Still forever

This might sound strange coming from the person who built it.

But I genuinely don't want you to become dependent on this theme.

I want the opposite.

I want Still to become the project that finally made everything click.

The one where Astro stopped feeling mysterious.

The one where components started making sense.

The one that gave you enough confidence to build whatever comes next.

If that happens...

Then Still has done its job.

## Please change everything

When people fork an open-source project, there's always a temptation to preserve it exactly as it is.

The colors.

The typography.

The layout.

The spacing.

I understand why.

It's easier.

But it's also a missed opportunity.

Still isn't precious.

It's a starting point.

Change the fonts.

Move sections around.

Delete components.

Invent new ones.

Throw away entire pages if they don't fit your needs.

Your website should gradually stop looking like mine.

## Keep what matters

Not every design decision deserves to survive.

Some exist because I liked them.

Others because they solved a particular problem.

As you customize Still, ask yourself a simple question.

> Does this still serve the kind of website I'm trying to build?

If the answer is no...

Remove it.

One of the nicest things about owning your own website is that nobody can tell you how it *should* look.

## Your writing matters more than your design

I know.

I'm a designer.

This sounds like heresy.

But after two decades of building websites, I've become convinced that people rarely return because your margins are perfect.

They return because you gave them something worth thinking about.

A thoughtful essay.

A beautiful photograph.

A useful tutorial.

A story they couldn't stop thinking about.

Design creates the conditions for that experience.

Content creates the experience itself.

Never confuse the two.

## Build slowly

The internet has a habit of making us feel behind.

Someone always has:

A prettier homepage.

A cleverer animation.

A bigger audience.

A shinier portfolio.

Forget all of that.

Your website doesn't need to be finished this weekend.

Or this month.

Or this year.

Mine certainly wasn't.

Some of my favorite websites have clearly been evolving for decades.

Let yours do the same.

## Contribute if you'd like

If you discover a bug...

Open an issue.

If you improve something...

Open a pull request.

If you have an idea that could make Still better for everyone...

I'd love to hear it.

One of the nicest things about open source is realizing that software doesn't have to be built alone.

Sometimes the best ideas arrive from complete strangers.

## Keep learning

Still teaches one particular way of building websites.

It isn't the only way.

Explore other Astro themes.

Read other people's code.

Learn CSS.

Learn accessibility.

Learn typography.

Learn how browsers actually work.

Every new skill makes the next project a little less intimidating.

## Build things that deserve to exist

I'd like to leave you with one last thought.

The web doesn't necessarily need another website.

It needs more people who genuinely care about what they publish.

If your website teaches something...

Shares something beautiful...

Documents your craft...

Makes someone laugh...

Or simply helps one other person...

Then it deserves to exist.

That's always been my philosophy.

Not to make more stuff.

To make **stuff that matters.**

## Thank you

If you've made it all the way here...

Thank you.

For downloading Still.

For reading the documentation.

For believing that the open web is still worth investing in.

I hope this theme saves you some time.

I hope it teaches you a few things.

And more than anything...

I hope it gives you a place that feels unmistakably yours.

Happy building.

— Sergio Camalich
