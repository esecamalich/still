It wasn't unusual for me to come across an area in the process of renovation, and to surprise myself not just by the extent of the demolished areas, but by the half-torn-down buildings –– still functional –– with their innards on display.

![Man overlooking construction site](/public/images/oceanPark-02.webp "Man overlooking construction site")

The sight of high rises was an ever present image in the background of the city. Luxury apartments, malls, rooftop bars with prime view spots. All the signs of a modern society, crammed together into a developer-friendly package.

![Housing development in the outskirts of Hanoi](/public/images/oceanPark-03.webp "Housing development in the outskirts of Hanoi")

Not much has changed since my initial days in Vietnam. If anything, the rate of development has increased to accommodate the country's massive economical and urbanization growth.

## Corporate Cities

![View of Mega Grand World Hanoi](/public/images/oceanPark-04.webp "View of Mega Grand World Hanoi")

Walking along the artificial river inside Mega Grand World Hanoi on a weekday, something felt out of place.

Lines of empty pastel-colored buildings surrounded us, motorized "gondolas" took tourists on a loop of the river, pop music blasted at full volume across all the area's speakers.

A few minutes earlier, we had driven by a grandiose university building, a man-made beach with sand brought up from the central coasts of Vietnam, a dutch windmill, an Eiffel Tower replica, and a whole assortment of staged scenes borrowed from some of the world's most famous Instagram spots.

![Entrance to VinUniversity](/public/images/oceanPark-05.webp "Entrance to VinUniversity")

![View of Ocean Park's fake beach](/public/images/oceanPark-06.webp "View of Ocean Park's fake beach")

![Tourists riding a gondola at Ocean Park](/public/images/oceanPark-07.webp "Tourists riding a gondola at Ocean Park")

Leaving aesthetics aside for a moment, my main thought as I walked through these streets was:

> All of this development –– dozens of apartment buildings across three sites, minimarkets, malls, electric cars, hospitals, schools & universities –– belong to a single company, VinGroup, a massive Vietnamese conglomerate.

It is being marketed to investors and consumers as a *world-class mega city*, a *resort paradise*. Smart, sustainable, and of course, great ROI.

To be fair, on paper, it's an idyllic place to live. Perfectly manicured and conveniently featured for most of our human needs. 

The question then is, what's wrong about this?

## How cities are born

Historically, cities were born organically, out of the chaos brought by the coming together of a dense population.

Though there have been examples of well-designed cities across history, most cities were not consciously "designed" at all. They emerged through long negotiations between geography, economy, culture, power, and everyday life.
